Hmm. Assert always terminates the program, so it's a good troubleshooting tool for stuff that should
be fixed (fail early and loudly) an debugging (it's telling that asserts can be turned off by the NDEBUG
macro). In case of problems that are beyond the control of the program, it's a given that they will
happen at least sometimes "in the wild" and then it's probably better to try to handle them in
a way that does not cause termination, but instead lets the program recover if at all possible and
finish doing what it was intended to do.

Examples of problems beyond control of the program:
- Bad user input
- Differences in the environment
- Interference from other programs or the operating system
